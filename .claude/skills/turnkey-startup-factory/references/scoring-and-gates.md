# Scoring, Forecasts, and Approval Gates

## Three Independent Ratings

Use a 1–10 scale. Provide one positive signal, one counter-signal, and a confidence level for every category. A score is an estimate, not a guarantee.

### Profit Potential

Answer: **Can this become an economically attractive business?**

| Subcriterion | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Pain and willingness to pay | 25% |
| Gross-margin potential | 20% |
| Customer-acquisition economics | 20% |
| Recurrence and expansion | 20% |
| Capital, operating, and regulatory burden, scored inversely | 15% |

| Score | Anchor |
| ---: | --- |
| 1–2 | No clear buyer or payment logic; structurally poor economics |
| 3–4 | Possible revenue but weak margin, acquisition, or execution profile |
| 5–6 | Plausible small-business economics with material assumptions untested |
| 7–8 | Strong pain, credible pricing, attractive delivery economics, and a testable acquisition path |
| 9–10 | Multiple validated signals and unusually strong economics; require sourced evidence |

### Popularity Potential

Answer: **Will enough intended users notice, try, share, or repeatedly use it?**

| Subcriterion | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Audience breadth and pain frequency | 20% |
| Observable demand or momentum | 20% |
| Differentiation and memorability | 20% |
| Retention, habit, or network potential | 20% |
| Distribution and shareability | 20% |

| Score | Anchor |
| ---: | --- |
| 1–2 | Tiny or inaccessible audience with weak relevance |
| 3–4 | Some interest but difficult discovery or weak distinction |
| 5–6 | Defined audience and plausible demand with limited evidence |
| 7–8 | Clear audience, visible demand, memorable story, and reachable channels |
| 9–10 | Exceptional sourced momentum plus strong organic distribution; use rarely |

### Ease of Use and Learning

Answer: **Can a typical target user understand the offer, begin quickly, and receive value with little friction?**

| Subcriterion | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Clarity and time to first value | 25% |
| Onboarding effort | 20% |
| Cognitive load | 20% |
| Integration and behavior change | 20% |
| Support and accessibility | 15% |

| Score | Anchor |
| ---: | --- |
| 1–2 | Expert-only, lengthy setup, or unclear value |
| 3–4 | Considerable training, integration, or workflow disruption |
| 5–6 | Learnable with guidance; some setup or behavior change |
| 7–8 | Clear, guided, fast to value, and manageable to adopt |
| 9–10 | Nearly self-explanatory with immediate value; confirm with user testing before awarding 10 |

## Ranking Formula

Calculate:

`ranking_score = profit × 0.40 + popularity × 0.35 + ease × 0.25`

Show all three category scores next to the composite. Break ties using, in order: higher confidence, higher profit score, cheaper seven-day validation, then lower regulatory burden.

## Build Gate

Build the full package only when every condition passes.

| Gate | Pass Condition |
| --- | --- |
| Composite | At least 7.0 |
| Category floor | No category below 6.0 |
| Confidence | At least two categories are Medium or High |
| Buyer | A named buyer and purchase trigger exist |
| Validation | A meaningful low-cost test can run within seven days |
| Fatal risk | No unresolved legal, safety, platform, fraud, privacy, or regulatory blocker |

If a concept fails, return a validation sprint. Do not adjust scores merely to create a winner.

## Three-Year Operating Summary

Label the forecast: **Management-based scenario; not audited actuals.** Do not call an operating cash proxy EBITDA, net income, or free cash flow.

Use model-specific demand drivers and include:

| Line | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Demand driver | State customers, subscriptions, events, transactions, locations, or another driver |
| Units or accounts | Derive annual volume from the demand driver |
| Average price or revenue per account | Label as sourced, tested, or assumed |
| Gross sales | Show formula |
| Direct costs | Include hosting, fulfillment, payment fees, direct support, labor, or product COGS |
| Other variable costs | Include commissions, refunds, travel, or transaction-specific items |
| Fixed operating expense | Include product, sales, administration, insurance, and required tools |
| Net operating cash before debt and tax | Gross sales minus listed operating costs |
| Net operating cash margin | Operating cash proxy divided by gross sales |

Use conservative, base, and upside cases when uncertain variables change the decision. Show sensitivity to the two highest-impact assumptions. If a figure lacks a source or test result, label it **assumption**.

## Approval Tiers

| Tier | Risk | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Low | Automatically research public information, select keywords, draft and score concepts, write copy, create local files, and build non-public prototypes. |
| 2 | Medium | Complete drafts but notify before consequential continuation, including production-ready integrations, collecting ordinary form data, or relying on material business assumptions. |
| 3 | High | Require explicit approval before publishing, purchasing, paying, sending outreach, posting publicly, submitting to an app store, collecting sensitive data, signing terms, or making legal or financial commitments. |

## Evidence and Integrity Rules

1. Label every key statement as sourced fact, calculation, assumption, or recommendation when ambiguity is possible.
2. Never fabricate market size, growth, conversion, churn, acquisition cost, or willingness-to-pay evidence.
3. Mark unsupported decision-critical items `TBD`.
4. Include the strongest counterargument for each concept.
5. Lower confidence if more than one-third of decision-critical inputs are `TBD`.
6. Preserve human control over public actions, spending, communications, personal data, and commitments.
